# WAVE2 independent review — consumer consistency and Platform ownership

**Review target:** the complete immutable `research/reviews/wave2-plan.md`, SHA-256 `28aebbb5b0c24231f2002e7aa2fb081d60e29cd98fe7a84e0173d55ab723a85a`.

**Verdict:** request changes for **two P1 consumer-protocol gaps** and **one P2 qualification gap**. No P0 found within this lens. The payment-side revisions, adverse-evidence barrier, separate subscription feed, and stable benefit keys are useful improvements. Their guarantees stop short of a complete consumer reduction/resolution protocol, however. A producer revision is an ingestion guard; it cannot also be the sole progress clock for independently owned business effects.

I fully read the supplied handoff, Q v1.1.2, the target plan, `research/platform-foundation-q.md`, `research/reliability.md`, and `research/provider-contracts.md`. I checked the retained Platform Commercial Access dossier and the relevant accepted ADR-0007 sections directly. I did not read peer WAVE2 reports or previous critic reports/journals, change the plan, implement code, access payment accounts, or spawn agents. The traces below are design counterexamples, not claims of reproduced runtime defects.

## C1 — P1: acknowledging a held consumer decision can make its later resolution impossible

**Exact location:** §5, “T3: передача владельцу прав/кредитов,” especially line 172; §6 line 190 (`pending_policy` and immutable policy metadata); §7 lines 198–202 (correlation hold and missing correction policy); §12 P5/P7. The only concrete audited resolution use case in §4 resolves an unknown **payment operation**, not a held consumer decision.

T3 permits a grant/correction only when the incoming producer evidence revision is strictly newer. Equal revision/same digest is a duplicate without effects. Meanwhile, the consumer must preserve `pending_policy` when an offer/period mapping, refund policy, or reversal correlation cannot be established. Durable acknowledgement is appropriate after recording that pending state, but the plan does not describe a second progress mechanism for it.

**Failure trace:**

1. Credit consumer receives transaction evidence revision 4 containing a completed purchase and an approved partial refund. Its historical offer is known, but no approved rule determines the correction after consumption.
2. It atomically stores the receipt, advances its evidence projection to 4, records `pending_policy`, and acknowledges. This follows the proposed safety behavior; it does not invent a negative balance.
3. The product owner subsequently approves the relevant historical policy, or an authorized reviewer establishes the missing reversal correlation. No new Paddle transaction/adjustment occurs, so the payment producer has no reason to emit revision 5.
4. Replaying revision 4 is a duplicate, and T3 expressly permits effects only for a strictly newer revision. The held grant/correction never progresses. The same problem affects an initially held purchase whose original-period mapping is later approved, even when no grant has ever existed.

**Consequence:** real completed purchases remain unfulfilled, or approved adverse evidence remains unapplied, indefinitely. Changing `policyVersion`, fabricating a payment revision, deleting the receipt, or adding a new consumer identity would evade the safety protocol and may create duplicate grants. Merely retaining a pending row is insufficient recovery.

**Minimal solution:** distinguish **evidence admission progress** from **consumer decision/effect progress**. Admission still obeys the current monotone producer-revision and digest rules. A consumer-owned `resolveHeldDecision`/redrive use case must be able to evaluate its latest saved evidence without a newer producer message. It authorizes and records the decision, expected local decision revision, historical policy/binding selection, actor or approval reference, and the exact evidence revisions evaluated. It then atomically commits the effect-or-still-pending state and consumer outbox under the same stable benefit key. Existing applied policy metadata remains immutable; resolving a held case must not reinterpret an already issued grant silently.

The consumer also needs a durable unresolved obligation or an explicit enumerable held-case API, so an acknowledgement does not remove the only work trigger. A generic callback retry is unnecessary. This can be a small owner-local operation and recovery query, with no new service, shared Unit of Work, or production ledger implementation.

**Planned assertions:** hold and acknowledge revision 4; approve the historical test policy without emitting another producer revision; resolve once and assert the intended effect. Crash before and after that local commit, replay the resolution and the original message, and assert no duplicate effect. Deliver revision 5 concurrently with resolution and require a CAS retry against the newer evidence rather than a stale decision. Repeat for “never granted,” “already granted and correction held,” and unresolved reversal correlation. An unrelated current catalog version must not change the selected historical policy.

This is an engineering recovery gap. The actual refund percentage, debt rule, late-payment eligibility, and credit quantity properly remain product decisions.

## C2 — P1: per-source revision guards do not protect the shared consumer consistency boundary

**Exact location:** §5 lines 159–172 (one complete snapshot per transaction, independent subscription aggregate, T3 lock keyed by producer aggregate); §6 lines 184–190 (late historical payments and several transactions mapping to one grant basis); §7 lines 196–202 (adjustment transitions and eventual corrections).

The T3 lock/CAS is keyed by `(producer namespace, aggregate type/id)`. The intended business effect is deliberately keyed differently. Two transaction aggregates may support the same intent/benefit or subscription/benefit/period. Commercial Access may also use both payment evidence and the independently revisioned subscription feed to make one commercial decision. Serializing each input projection therefore does not serialize all inputs to that decision.

**Failure trace A — two monetary aggregates, one benefit:** transaction A and transaction B both complete for one monthly benefit/period. A produces the first grant; B is retained as a second real charge and anomaly, as required. Later A has an approved refund while a newer B observation is processed concurrently. The two handlers pass different aggregate revision guards. One evaluates a correction using its view of A; the other evaluates issuance/support using its view of B. The unique grant key stops a second original grant, but says nothing about which payment supports the existing allocation, whether B can replace A's support, which refund affects it, or whether an already recorded reduction must be restored. Independent negative/restoration records can still race despite the single grant row.

The plan need not select the commercial answer. A fixture policy can designate a particular causal payment, hold all duplicated-payment cases for review, or evaluate a documented support set. It must select and persist one such protocol. Otherwise delivery order becomes an accidental policy, and “one grant” is compatible with an incorrect spendable result.

**Failure trace B — transaction versus subscription stream:** assume the test policy explicitly allows a benefit only while the latest local commercial lifecycle state permits it. A payment handler reads the locally saved active subscription evidence, then pauses. A subscription-only cancellation handler applies its own newer subscription revision and the corresponding commercial restriction. The payment handler resumes and commits an entitlement decision based on its earlier read. Its transaction evidence revision is still current, so the stated T3 CAS succeeds. Neither comparing unrelated revision numbers nor atomically storing that transaction's receipt detects the stale lifecycle dependency.

This trace does not demand universal immediate access revocation on cancellation. It shows that **whatever** approved policy depends on both sources needs its entire read set protected. The accepted Platform boundary already requires Commercial Access to own commercial Subscription and Entitlement truth; an incoming payment cannot substitute its own source clock for the commercial aggregate's concurrency control.

**Consequence:** duplicate-payment refunds can reduce the wrong allocation, correction/restoration can depend on delivery timing, or a late payment decision can overwrite a more recent lifecycle-dependent commercial decision. All individual message receipts can be valid, and all per-source revisions monotone, while the product outcome is wrong.

**Minimal solution:** extend T3's consumer guidance with an owner-local consistency boundary for the **affected decision**, in addition to the per-source ingestion projection. For credit effects, serialize/CAS a stable benefit-basis record; for Commercial Access, serialize/CAS its own appropriate commercial aggregate. Before calculating an effect, load the latest relevant local evidence under that boundary, or validate an explicit expected read set/decision revision at commit. Persist which source aggregates and revisions contributed. Never read or lock payment-owner tables from either consumer.

For a benefit supported by several monetary aggregates, retain their contributions and a pinned selection/hold decision. Maintain enough applied-outcome state to distinguish “never issued,” “issued,” “reduced,” and “restored.” Compute a transition from that recorded outcome under the selected policy, rather than treating every newer snapshot as another refund delta. Give each actual local transition a stable effect identity; evidence revision, policy version, or reverse-action arrival alone is not an extra grant entitlement. A held-before-grant refund followed by rejection/reversal may become one original grant under the approved policy; it must not first manufacture a debit or compensation for a grant that never existed.

This is a small consumer protocol, not a requirement to implement general accounting or automatic provider netting. Unqualified reversal pairs remain held, exactly as §7 allows. The key addition is that the held decision and subsequent applied state have a single owner and a concurrency rule across every contributing source.

**Planned assertions:** run two real consumer connections against the same basis with distinct transaction aggregate IDs; interleave duplicate completion, full refund of each payment, and a later observation of the other payment. Under one explicitly documented fixture support policy, assert the same outcome for every delivery order, both financial facts retained, and no duplicated correction/restoration. Separately pause a paid handler after reading subscription evidence, commit cancellation/unsupported-plan evidence, then resume it. Require a retry/reduction against current local state, with no stale entitlement overwrite. Include cancellation-before-old-completion and old-completion-before-cancellation; do not compare subscription revision 7 numerically with transaction revision 2.

## C3 — P2: the sample acceptance criterion can pass while credit/access outcomes are wrong

**Exact location:** §12 P5 line 301, P6 line 303, and mandatory cases at lines 307–309. P5's stated result is “one saved grant and correct monetary history” after retries/crashes. P6 makes a separate credit fixture optional. The enumerated adverse scenarios do not state the expected **consumer outcome** for them.

**Failure trace:** a sample saves one grant row, correctly preserves the original completed payment and refund history, then applies the same approved refund again when another evidence revision repeats that adjustment. Alternatively, a refund-first sample stores a negative correction although it never issued credits. Both can still have exactly one saved grant and correct payment history. A sample can also reuse payment amounts as entitlement fields and appear to complete the happy path without demonstrating the separate owner boundary required by the handoff.

**Consequence:** the claimed end-to-end qualification can pass without proving the reusable library's central consumer promise. Developers copying the examples receive working HTTP/GM wiring but have to invent the critical state transition and recovery rules themselves. In particular, retaining one immutable grant is not the same assertion as maintaining the correct current spendable allocation or commercial capability.

**Minimal solution:** require at least one small **separate, nonproduction credit consumer fixture** and one commercial-access-style fixture, with independently owned receipts/effects and a short explicit test policy. It may use symbolic quantities and deliberately hold partial-refund or ambiguous reversal cases. No commercial prices, production credit ledger, or new Platform package need be approved or created. Both disposable host examples should exercise the same public delivery/receive contract; they need not duplicate a large ledger implementation.

For each relevant scenario, specify expected granted quantity, held quantity/state, applied correction/restoration transitions, separate included-quota/top-up classification, and commercial lifecycle/capability result. Keep payment history assertions separately. Independent test expectations must not be computed by the reducer under test, consistent with Q's verification guidance.

**Planned assertions:** add a compact fixture matrix for initial purchase, renewal, top-up, late collection, refund-before-grant, repeated approved refund in a newer full snapshot, initially approved adjustment, rejected pending refund, and qualified versus held reversal correlation. Assert retained grant identity **and** final effect state, correction count, and pending-resolution obligation. Run crash/replay through both application integrations. Verify that the commercial fixture stores capabilities/restrictions rather than an AI balance and that the payment fixture writes neither consumer's tables.

## Boundaries that should remain unchanged

The plan correctly preserves app-local buyer authority, stable account/environment namespaces, null-to-observed customer handling, and denial of customer-wide portal authority from an email match or one subscription binding (§§3, 4, 8). I found no additional blocker in those contracts. Do not replace them with a customer-per-app assumption or treat portal deep links as isolation.

Platform ownership is also correctly described. Retained ADR-0007, “Commercial Access boundary” and “Cross-context and external boundaries,” and the Commercial Access dossier reserve commercial rights while excluding credits/payment/accounting and forbidding shared transactions. The proposed fixes remain inside each consumer's own transaction. They neither materialize the unaccepted commercial package nor invent an accepted Commercial Accounting context. No second provider, shared cross-context SQL/UoW, mandatory gateway, or approved product policy is needed to close these findings.
